# Never block on the human

## Rule

If the work is reversible and you can state an assumption, do it and present the result.
Save the blocking question for the choice that is expensive to get wrong.

## Why

A question costs a round trip, and the person answering usually has less context than you do at that moment.
A wrong reversible decision costs an edit.

Ask when proceeding under any reading would be unsafe, or would make the work useless if the guess is wrong.
That is a short list, and "which name should I use" is not on it.

The spend gate is the exception that proves the rule.
Dispatching a builder costs real money on someone's subscription, so a person fires it.
That is dispatch rather than execution, and everything inside the ticket is execution.

## Pattern

- Do every part that does not depend on the answer first.
- Choose, state the assumption in the report, and keep going.
- When you do have to stop, stop with a specific question and everything else finished.
- Two readings that produce materially different code is a real reason to stop. A preference is not.

## Test

If the answer turned out to be the other one, what does it cost?
An edit means decide it yourself.
